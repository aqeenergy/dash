# PSI-9 · EHD — Electrically Driven Liquid Film Flow Boiling in the Absence of Gravity

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Sub-area:** Electrohydrodynamics
- **Hardware:** Electrically driven radial heat pipe
- **Flight platform:** Parabolic Flight
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** EHD · electric conduction · electric fields · boiling · Reduced Gravity · Electrodrodynamics · dielectrophoresis · critical heat flux · heat transfer · Presence of Gravity

## Objective

a-Provide fundamental understanding of the combined electrically driven (based on EHD conduction phenomenon) liquid film flow and dielectroporetic vapor extraction mechanism in the presence of phase change (liquid to vapor) in the absence of gravity.

b-Provide a phenomenological foundation for the future development of electric field based two phase thermal management systems, leveraging EHD engineering advantages to develop systems of arbitrary mass flow requirements and geometries.

## Hypothesis

Boiling is not acceptable in the absence of gravity unless the generated vapor bubbles can be expelled from the boiling surface and fresh liquid can be pumped onto the hot surface through some mechanism. EHD conduction pumping has the capability of pumping thin liquid films toward the heat source where the boiling takes place, with sufficient inertia to begin expelling bubbles from the heated surface at low heat fluxes. Furthermore, applying an EHD Dielectrophoretic (DEP) extraction force near the boiling surface guarantees the repulsion of vapor bubbles away from the heated surface even at high heat fluxes. Finally, the combined EHD driven liquid film flow boiling and DEP extraction force are expected to sustain and enhance the critical heat flux in the absence of gravity. Combining EHD conduction and DEP effects is therefore critical to the success of the ISS experiment.

## Approach

As vapor bubbles cover the surface and grow or coalesce, the modes of heat transfer will be limited to conduction and possibly radiation. To avoid this and to enable boiling to take place, EHD-driven liquid film will be fed from the condenser section towards the heater. The inertia of liquid film flow along with DEP extraction force will repel vapor bubbles that have formed on the heater surface. In order to study the competing effects of flow inertia/DEP extraction force vs. bubble behavior in zero gravity, one needs to understand EHD generated liquid film flow and the DEP mechanism. The flow inertia is a function of applied EHD potential, electrode design, liquid film thickness and working fluid properties. The DEP extraction force is also a function of its corresponding electrode design, the spacing between its electrode and heated surface, and the applied potential for a given working fluid. Thus, the controlling parameters for this study are applied EHD conduction pumping potential, applied DEP potential, heat flux, and liquid film height for the selected working fluid and electrode designs.  The electrode designs for EHD conduction pumping and DEP mechanism are based on our previous numerical and experimental work. Therefore, the electrode dimensions, design or distance from the heater surface will not need to be varied during this study.

## Research impacts

This multi-physics proposal will provide basic scientific understanding about competing physical phenomena of flow inertia and bubble dynamics in electrically driven liquid film flow boiling in the presence of electric bubble extraction force (i.e., dielectrophoretic force) in microgravity. The electrically driven liquid film flow boiling data aboard ISS will determine the critical heat flux, onset of boiling, absence of hysteresis, and bubble characteristics in the absence of gravity.There are a number of potential terrestrial and space applications for this electrohydrodynamic (EHD) driven two-phase heat transport device in all scales (from macro-scale to truly micro-scales). For example, this device can be used to effectively remove heat from electronics in micro- and zero-gravity environment for NASA's future space stations and long term missions. It can also be used in satellites (e.g., embedded system into satellite's multi-functional structure).Terrestrially, it can be used in high intensity electronics cooling as well as in various phase change devices. To achieve these applications, the basic scientific understanding about competing physical phenomena of flow inertia and bubble dynamics in electrically driven liquid film flow boiling in the presence of electric bubble extraction force (i.e.,dielectrophoretic force) in microgravity is needed.
