# PSI-186 · CGH — Coupled Growth in Hypermonotectics

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Solidification
- **Hardware:** Advanced Gradient Heating Facility (AGHF)
- **Flight platform:** Space Shuttle
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** immiscible alloys · Interfacial Stability · Microgravity · hypermonotectic · Coupled Growth

## Objective

The overall objective of the Coupled Growth in Hypermonotectics is to understand the fundamental physics controlling solidification in immiscible alloy systems. Specific experimental goals were (1) to show that production of an aligned fibrous microstructure is possible in hypermonotectic alloys by achieving interfacial stability and steady state coupled growth through microgravity processing, (2) to determine the influence of alloy composition and growth velocity on microstructure, and (3) to determine the growth velocity and composition range over which interface stability is possible in a hypermonotectic alloy system.

## Hypothesis

This investigation aimed to answer fundamental unanswered questions concerning solidification of immiscible alloys, namely: (a) is it possible to solidify a hypermonotectic alloy under steady state interfacially stable conditions to produce a fibrous microstructure, (b) can a simple supercooling approach predict stability limits, (c) does extensive diffusion in the liquid phases result in morphological effects not observed in eutectic systems, and (d) what role does surface tension driven flow play at the solidification front.

## Approach

The CGH experiment investigation flew aboard the Life and Microgravity Spacelab (LMS) mission during the summer of 1996. Processing conditions
were controlled in an attempt to force the production of an aligned fibrous phase in the microstructure of processed samples. The Advanced Gradient Heating Facility (AGHF) was used to directionally solidify the immiscible alloys. Aluminum (Al)-Indium (In) alloys were studied and specialized aluminum nitride (AlN) ampoules were used in order to control several undesirable effects that are sometimes observed during low gravity processing. Three alloy compositions, namely 17.3 weight % In monotectic, and 18.5 and 19.7 weight percent In hypermonotectic alloys were processed during this mission. A thermal gradient of 67 C/cm was achieved and all flight samples were processes at a translation velocity of 1 micrometer/s. Ground based control samples were subsequently processed under conditions identical to those of two of the flight samples in order to allow a direct comparison of the results.

## Research impacts

Many alloys in immiscible systems show great promise for potential applications in areas that include superconductors, magnetic materials, catalysts, and electrical contacts. For example, some immiscible alloys may be used to produce filters that can remove micron-sized particles for medical applications. Other alloys are expected to exhibit superconducting characteristics or have unusual magnetic properties.
