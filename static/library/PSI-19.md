# PSI-19 · ACE-TR — Advanced Colloids Experiment with Temperature Control - R [Ellipsoid]

- **Research area:** Complex Fluids - Soft Matter
- **Sub-area:** Colloids
- **Hardware:** ACE-TR with 2 Sample Modules (S/N R001 and R002) Light Microscopy Module (LMM) Fluids Integrated Rack (FIR)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Colloids · Ellipsoid · nematic ordering · microgravity · Confocal microscopy · hedgehog

## Objective

The objective of the ACE-TR (Ellipsoid) experiment was to image and document the self-assembly of complex 3D structures formed from small elliptical colloidal particles suspended in a fluid. Temperature gradients were varied with the objective to explore the effect of thermophoresis on colloidal crystallization. The goal was to crystallize a metastable colloidal liquid to study how the orientation and periodicity in particle arrangement evolves in different regions of the phase diagram of elliptical colloidal particles. The objective was to understand the effects of gravity on colloidal self-assembly, test predictions of current theories for soft materials, and develop novel techniques that can be applied to combine materials with  different densities in additive manufacturing. The expected outcome has the potential to make novel colloidal materials in a Low-Earth orbit and return them to Earth.

## Hypothesis

Despite the fundamental and technological importance, the understanding of colloidal crystallization is still elusive. This investigation hypothesizes that there is interrelation between processes driving orientation and periodicity in particle arrangement.  Comparing the outcome of the ACE-T11 (Hard Spheres) and ACE-TR (Ellipsoids) experiments should help elucidate this interrelation. This will enable a better understanding at the particle level of how a disordered arrangement of elliptical particles self-organizes into a 3D ordered structure.

## Approach

ACE-TR will view collections of temperature-controlled, fluorescent-core micron-sized colloidal particles. It uses two different sets of ellipsoids, each in their own sample cells. These have aspect ratios of either 1x2.8 or 1x4.5 microns. The particles are small enough that 50 of them could be placed end to end across the width of a fine human hair; yet big enough that the particles and the structures they form can be photographed with visible light of a confocal microscope. The observations will be done using a 3D confocal microscope at the particle level for sufficiently high particle concentrations; in some samples, we will attempt to control the desired concentration by applying a temperature gradient, to see if the particle number density will adjust itself as a function of temperature throughout the sample cell. ImageJ software and internally developed MATLAB functions and scripts were used for processing and quantification of images collected.

## Research impacts

ACE-TR reveals several fundamental and practical aspects of soft matter science with potential applications on Earth. Self-assembly processes are crucial to making functional materials and devices from small particles. Improved design and assembly of structures fabricated in microgravity may have use in a variety of fields from medicine to opto-electronics on Earth. Ultimately, the ability to design and build functional structures based on colloids will allow new devices for chemical energy, communication, and photonics, including photonic materials to control and manipulate light. The rapidly growing fields of 3D printing and additive manufacturing rely on the assembly and sintering of particle aggregates and the preparation of high-density slurries and pastes of different colloidal materials and with different rheological and mechanical properties.  These ellipsoids may  behave like liquid crystals when they self-assemble and their possible defects (hedgehogs) may also be visible.
