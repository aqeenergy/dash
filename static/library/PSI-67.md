# PSI-67 — Enhancement and Verification of Quantitative Phase-Field Crystal Modeling using NASA-PSI Coarsening in Solid- Liquid Mixtures Experiments Data

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Solidification, Coarsening
- **Project type:** Ground Investigation
- **Keywords:** coarsening · Molecular Dynamics · Solid - Liquid Mixtures · Binary Alloys · solidification · Phase Field Crystals · Ostwald Ripening · Interatomic Potential

## Objective

The key objectives of this investigation were to develop 1) a binary Molecular Dynamics (MD) interatomic potential for Pb-Sn system, 2) Phase Field Crystal (PFC) models for coarsening of solid-liquid mixtures; 3) a highly-parallel semi-implicit spectral and operator splitting numerical algorithm to use on high performance computing facilities that will enable the necessary large-scale PFC simulations (comparable to CSLM data length-scale); (4) Data mining of NASA-PSI Coarsening in Solid-Liquid Mixtures (CSLM), CSLM-2, CSLM-2R, and CSLM-3 experiments and using them in series of simulations in the developed computational tool for enhancement and verification of the proposed modeling framework.

## Hypothesis

Modified-embedded-atom (MEAM) interatomic potential and a one mode phase field crystal (1PFC) model can be utilized to simulate the kinetics of coarsening of solid phases in solid-liquid mixtures.

## Approach

The proposed multi-scale modeling framework is established around two atomistic computational material model of phase-field crystals (PFC) and molecular dynamics (MD). PFC is a unique computational model at diffusive time scale with the capability of simulating solid-liquid processes with atomistic resolutions while MD acts on the time scale of atomistic vibrations.
A novel approach of modified-embedded-atom (MEAM) interatomic potential was adopted to obtain reliable interatomic potentials for Pb-Sn binary systems near or at the melting point (MP). The potentials were used to calculate relevant properties such as latent heat, expansion in melting, liquid structure factor, solid-liquid interface stiffness, solidus and liquidus lines, and solid-liquid interfacial free energy. The kinetics of coarsening in solid-liquid mixtures (Ostwald ripening of solid domains in the liquid phase) was simulated using a one mode phase field crystal (1PFC) model. The simulation results were compared to experimental data previously obtained in a microgravity environment. Simulations were carried out as a function of the difference in chemical potential between Pb and Sn. ## Research impacts

The proposed computational tool verified/quantified based on CSLM data will establish a virtual laboratory that can be used to study coarsening as well as other material processing phenomena on Earth involving with solid-liquid coexistence; e.g., rapid solidification and grain growth. Since computational models are highly repeatable at lower cost, the proposed modeling framework will result in accelerated material process development and certification. Solid-liquid coexistence is the initial step in many manufacturing technologies such as casting, liquid infiltration, and additive manufacturing. Hence, this modeling framework can be used to investigate the relations between these manufacturing processes and the material properties in order to optimize/control the manufacturing processes parameters. In addition, the results of this research will be disseminated to the public in forms of peer-reviewed articles, scientific reports, a project-specific website, and data uploaded to NASA-PSI website.
