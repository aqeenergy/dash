# PSI-191 · ACE-T5 — Advanced Colloids Experiment-Temperature 5

- **Research area:** Complex Fluids - Soft Matter
- **Sub-area:** Bijels, Colloids
- **Hardware:** Fluids Integrated Rack (FIR) Light Microscopy Module (LMM)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** bijel · Marangoni convection · spinodal decomposition · self assembly · microgravity

## Objective

The overarching goal of the project was to gain a better fundamental understanding of the relationship between bijel morphology, rheology, mechanical stability, and processability through combined ground-based and microgravity experiments and theoretical calculations.

## Hypothesis

This investigation hypothesizes that interparticle interactions have a profound impact on the rheology of bijel particulate suspensions and attempts to answer the fundamental scientific question as to how the various forces at the colloidal scale are affected by Marangoni convection in microgravity. The nature of interparticle interactions and its impact on rheology has profound implications for the processability of bijels and the ability to transform them into functional materials.

## Approach

Mixtures of propylene glycol/nitroethane (PG/NE) at their critical composition were prepared. Partially hydrophobic silica particles were suspended in the PG/NE mixture and the suspensions were filled in different cuvettes. On orbit, these cuvettes were heated to 25C, mixed in-situ, and then quenched from 25C to 0C. Following the quench, an imaging protocol was used to collect a stack of 100 images in the Z direction at a spacing of 5 mm.

## Research impacts

The ability to design and fabricate the unique 3-dimensional bijel structures opens a wide spectrum of new structures for applications that can benefit from synthesis of hierarchically
porous monoliths and composites with a spinodal-like microstructure in which every constituent phase is naturally continuous throughout the entire sample volume. This type of tailored porous
microstructures can improve the physical properties and performance of materials used in a number of space and terrestrial applications. Space exploration applications include improved energy
storage batteries and fuel cells, and catalytic microreactors for lower mass and volume designs in spacecraft life support and ISRU systems. There is a diverse array of potential terrestrial applications in electrochemical energy conversion and storage, tissue engineering, sensing, microstructured composites with optimized mechanical properties for MEMS, microfluidic heat exchangers and catalytic reformers, separations.
