# PSI-78 · IVGEN — IntraVenous Fluid GENeration for Exploration Missions

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Hardware:** Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** water treatment · medical equipment · fluid flow

## Objective

The main goal of this experiment is to demonstrate in a space environment, a prototype system that produces USP grade SWI, and mixes the SWI with sterile USP sodium chloride to produce a homogeneous Normal Saline IV fluid. The experiment shall also demonstrate at least one bubble removal apparatus, as this is a critical step to prevent delivering harmful gas emboli to patients when the deployed system is used. The system will utilize a single flow rate, determined by the maximum volume/hour required for a worst-case-scenario medical event.

## Hypothesis

It is hypothesized that a prototype system, utilizing deionization, magnetic stirring, and bubble separation techniques, can effectively produce USP-compliant normal saline solution from readily available spacecraft potable water sources in a microgravity environment.

## Approach

The IVGEN investigation employs a comprehensive and well-structured approach to demonstrate and validate the feasibility of producing IV fluids in space. The approach encompasses a combination of on-orbit experimentation, ground-based testing, and meticulous data analysis, as summarized below:

 On-Orbit Experimentation: The core of the investigation involves a flight experiment onboard the International Space Station (ISS), utilizing a prototype IV fluid generation system. This experiment simulates the conditions and constraints of a space mission, allowing for direct testing of the system's performance in a microgravity environment.

 Ground-Based Testing: Prior to the flight experiment, a rigorous ground-based test program is conducted to:
    -Concept Verification: Validate the design and performance of key system components, such as the bubble separators and mixers, in both normal and reduced gravity environments.
    -Input Water Analysis: Characterize the quality of potential water sources, including ISS potable water and various CEV water sources, to ensure the system's compatibility and effectiveness.
    -Temperature Effects: Assess the impact of varying temperatures on the system's performance, particularly on the DI cartridge and the conductivity sensors.

 Data Acquisition and Analysis: During the on-orbit experiment, a comprehensive dataset is collected, including:
    -Environmental Data: Temperature, pressure, and acceleration measurements to understand the system's operating environment and potential impact on performance.
    -System Performance Data: Conductivity, flow rate, and image data to assess the purification, mixing, and bubble removal processes.

 Post-Flight Sample Analysis: The two 1.5-liter bags of NS produced on orbit are returned to Earth for rigorous testing by a certified laboratory, ensuring the produced IV fluid meets USP standards.

 Data Interpretation and Scaling: The collected data is meticulously analyzed to:
    -Validate System Performance: Confirm the successful production of USP-compliant NS solution in microgravity.
    -Identify Challenges and Limitations: Assess potential issues and areas for improvement based on observed system behavior.
    -Enable System Scaling: Utilize the data to inform the design and development of a larger, operational system capable of meeting the needs of future exploration missions.

## Research impacts

Due to mass and volume limitations, space vehicles cannot carry sufficient IV fluid for medical contingencies. A filtering and mixing system that can make IV fluid in situ would provide the treatment capability without the mass and volume constraints. IVGEN was designed and will be tested to meet that need. IVGEN technology could be used on Earth to generate IV fluid in Third World countries where medical resources are limited.
