# PSI-45 · CVB-2 — Constrained Vapor Bubble-2

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Related investigations:** PSI-46
- **Keywords:** Marangoni flow · Heat pipes

## Objective

The primary objective of CVB-2 is to determine the overall interfacial stability, characterize the hydrodynamics, measure heat transfer coefficients, and determine the overall conductance of a complex fluid (94% Pentane/6% Isohexane) in a microgravity environment.

## Hypothesis

In microgravity environments, the introduction of a higher boiling point, higher surface tension component into a low-boiling-point fluid within a constrained vapor bubble suppresses temperature-induced Marangoni convection, thereby enhancing heat transfer performance and system stability.

## Approach

The Constrained Vapor Bubble (CVB) Experiment was conducted on the International Space Station (ISS) and the temperature data have been used to analyze the heat transfer mechanisms operating within the quartz cuvette for 40 mm and 30 mm modules. Even with the large millimeter dimensions of the CVB, interfacial forces dominate in these exceedingly small Bond Number systems with small viscous losses. A comparison of the modeling results obtained using a simple one-dimensional analysis of the temperature fields found with the dry and partially liquid pentane filled cuvettes allowed the phase change and radiation fields to be determined in both microgravity and earth gravity. Unique visual observation of the shape of the liquid flow field through a microscope enhanced the understanding of the temperature and vapor pressure [1],[2] measurements. The internal heat transfer processes were very complicated, multidimensional, and the length of the closed system strongly affected the average internal heat transfer processes. Internal radiation was shown to be more significant than originally anticipated as well as the effect of Marangoni convection. The competing processes of capillary flow driving liquid from the cold to the hot end and Marangoni flow driving liquid from the hot end to the cold end resulted in the formation of a central drop and a pinch point in the film thickness profile. The results also indicate that the study of the constrained vapor bubble in an open loop heat pipe configuration would allow the passive bubble more freedom to respond.

## Research impacts

Most liquids have a surface tension that decreases as temperature rises. This causes fluid to flow in the direction of higher surface tension, a phenomenon known as Marangoni flow. This reduces the effectiveness of a heat pipe, because liquid cannot return to the hot end. CVB-2 investigates whether a mixture of two fuels (pentane and isohexane) that can reduce Marangoni flow and improve the efficiency of the CVB system on the International Space Station. In addition, the investigation provides insight into how heat transfer functions in microgravity. Most satellites use heat pipes to keep critical parts cool, and improved understanding of fluid mixtures could enable future heat pipes that are smaller, lighter and less expensive to launch. A wide range of high-performance devices must be perpetually cooled in order to function, from supercomputers to personal laptops and LED light bulbs. Fluid mixtures circulating through heat pipes can passively dissipate heat, using less energy than fans or cooling systems. But heat pipe transport systems are sensitive to small differences in temperature, pressure, and gravity. Studying how fluid mixtures and heat pipes function in microgravity provides insight into the fundamental principles of thermodynamics. This also provides insight into how mixtures of fluids behave during boiling, condensation, evaporation, distillation, and stripping. These processes are used in a variety of industries and environmental cleanup.
