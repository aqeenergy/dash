# PSI-83 · MICS-MVP — Microgravity Investigation of Cement Solidification - Multi-use Variable Gravity Platform

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Cement Solidification
- **Hardware:** Multi-use Variable-gravity Platform (MVP).
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** tricalcium aluminate · cement · variable gravity · gypsum · hydration

## Objective

The purpose of the MICS-MVP is to investigate and understand the complex process of cement solidification as a function of variable gravity and to advance the use of
concrete materials for extraterrestrial infrastructure. MICS-MVP will identify the differences between the hydration reaction and microstructural formation at the different gravity levels.

## Hypothesis

It is hypothesized that under variable reduced gravity conditions different transport phenomena such as buoyancy, sedimentation, and convection are minimized and a
more diffusion dominant transport will lead to varying degree of uniformity in distribution ofphases, porosity, and crystal morphology. A different microstructure is expected  to form in at different gravity levels which possibly leads to different physical properties of the reduced gravity hardened cement paste with respect to that hardened in terrestrial gravity (1g).

## Approach

Two different mixture combinations of with water and gypsum and tricalcium silicate (C3S) with water were hydrated under 5 different gravity levels, namely, Earth's
gravity, microgravity, and simulated gravity levels of the Moon, Mars, and a statistical point at 0.7g.  The different components were each placed in opposing compartments of a plastic pouch and separated by a middle burstable seal. To burst the seal, the water filled compartment was rolled up from the outside edge towards the middle until enough forward pressure was achieved to burst the seal. The water is then completely moved into the compartment containing the tricalcium aluminate (C3A) or tricalcium silicate (C3S) and the paste is mixed by a rubber spatula and/or kneaded by hand to reach a homogeneous state. A plastic clip is then attached to help consolidate the paste at one end of the pouch. This mixing procedure generally took a few minutes per sample. The ISS itself provides a microgravity environment, whereas the gravity levels of extraterrestrial bodies that are less than Earths were simulated with centrifugal forces within the Multi-use Variable-gravity Platform (MVP). Samples at different gravity levels were hydrated for different lengths of time, following which the centrifuge was shut off and the samples continued to hydrate till they were returned to earth. The samples were characterized for pore size, pore distribution, portlandite size distribution, crystal morphology, and phase content.

## Research impacts

The MICS investigation directly addresses the high priority need for lightweight space structures for habitats that protect crew. Results from this investigation enable the characterization of microstructural development in the hardening of cement paste and improve understanding of crystal growth and hydration kinetics, phase formations, pore distribution, and material properties. Also, MICS can increase understanding and control of the crystal growth kinetics, and morphology, to improve concrete properties. Improved cement properties can lead to improved concrete properties, durability, and lower CO2 emission.
