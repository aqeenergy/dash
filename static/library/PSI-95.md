## Objective

Plasma crystals are a new kind of matter, rediscovered in 1994. They form under certain conditions in a complex (dusty) plasma. There, the electrically charged dust particles arrange in a regular macroscopic crystal lattice. This structure allows for an investigation of the properties of condensed matter on the kinetic level. This means that basic processes, such as melting, can be followed by observing the motion of individual particles. PK-3 will give investigators a better understanding of plasma in space and will determine the critical points for the plasma.

Gravity plays an important role for the structure of plasma crystals. In microgravity large 3-dimensional plasma crystals can be grown and studied in detail on the most fundamental, the kinetic level. Complex plasmas can be seen as model systems for the investigation of fundamental phenomena in liquids and crystals and provide information on the motion of single particles (“atoms”). This provides new insights into the field of condensed matter.

## Approach

PK-3 Plus is a symmetrical driven radio-frequency plasma discharge with special features for the investigation of complex plasmas under microgravity conditions. As a second generation laboratory, PK-3 Plus provides major new possibilities for these investigations due to its design improvements relative to the first long-term experiment PKE-Nefedov. The PK-3 Plus apparatus allows investigations at neutral gas pressures between 0.05 - 2.5 millibar and radio frequency (rf) power of 0.01 - 1 W. The complex plasma can consist of monodisperse particles in a size range from 1 - 20 micrometers. Up to six particle sizes can be added to the experimental volume. It is possible to change the number of particles, the composition of particles, the plasma conditions and the neutral gas pressure during one experiment. The particle cloud can be excited by an electrical low frequency signal on the electrodes (0.1 - 100 Hz at a maximum amplitude of 50 V) or by a low frequency modulation of the rf-amplitude in different wave forms (sinusoidal, square, pulse, etc.).

The PK-3 Plus investigation has two major pieces of equipment: the experimental block, and the telescience system (TS). The research will be performed on the ISS inside the plasma chamber situated inside of the experimental block. The experimental block is attached via a tube to the space environment to produce the prevacuum for turbomolecular pump, which can produce pressure in the plasma chamber less than 10-5 millibar.

The TS is the computer in which the chamber conditions can be altered via actuators in the experimental block and the storage unit for the data collected. This PK3 Plus laboratory has state of the art hardware and software, and provides better diagnostics than previous hardware. The PK-3 Plus laboratory was operated on the Russian segment of ISS by Russian cosmonauts mostly.

Typical experimental campaigns have 3 or 4 runs to meet the requirements of the investigator. There are two modes, automatic and manual, for this investigation. During the manual mode, crew time will be required to complete the investigation. Experimenters are following live video transmission from the experimental block in the control center in Korolev guiding the cosmonaut via audio contact for experiment parameters optimization. Full set Recorded videos and housekeeping data are to be returned on hard drives via Soyuz capsule.

The experiments run on three or four consecutive days during single experimental campaign. Some days areon automatic, as mentioned above, and some days are manual operations, depending on the cosmonaut availability and investigators plans. On automatic days, the machine can be left alone to run, passively taking measurements of the complex plasma behaviors under microgravity. On the manual day, there will be a cosmonaut present, adjusting settings to achieve the required states of the plasma. The experiment is expected to take approximately 90 minutes each day.

## Research impacts

99% of the visible matter in the universe is in the plasma state and dust is ubiquitous, too. Thus so called dusty plasmas can be found in interstellar media, in planetary forming discs, in cometary atmospheres, in Saturn rings, etc. The results from the PK-3 Plus laboratory on the ISS on the charging of the microparticles/dust and their interaction with each other and the surrounding plasma, the so-called basic fundamental questions, are very helpful in the understanding of dusty plasmas in space. Additionally, complex plasmas are models for liquid and crystalline systems providing new insights into the formation and behavior of condensed matter.
