## Objective

The objective is to study coarsening in solid-liquid mixtures at low volume fractions of solid. The topology and morphology of these dendritic mixtures will be determined using three-dimensional reconstructions and phase field simulations that employ these reconstructions as initial conditions will provide insights into the mechanisms of coarsening.  These low volume fractions will enable the science team to examine coarsening of single or a few dendrites, something that is impossible on the ground.  These studies will provide insights into the dynamics of morphological evolution of these systems and will point the way to the formulation of models of this technologically important coarsening process.

## Hypothesis

The fundamental questions that this investigation attempts to answer are as follows:
Does the Interface Shape Distribution (ISD) vary with position from the tip of the dendrite? If so, as is likely the case, does the ISD scale with time, as is usually the case during coarsening? How important is the spatial distribution of curvature in setting the evolution of the structure? How do diffusional interactions between the secondary, and perhaps tertiary, arms on different dendrites affect the evolution of the system? How does the ISD evolve when there are multiple dendrites in the system? Does scaling exist?

## Approach

The CSLM-4 approach and hardware was essentially identical to the predecessor CSLM-3 investigations The solid volume fraction of the 4 Pb-Sn sample for CSLM-4 was 0.3. Isothermal holding times between 10 minutes and 15 hours were employed. After the requisite holding times the samples were quenched using pressurized gas. Upon receipt of the samples they were analyzed using three-dimensional reconstructions of the microstructure. The 3D reconstructions was used to determine the interfacial curvatures, the genus and the spatial distribution of curvature in the system. The experimentally measured structures were used to compare phase field simulations and experiment.

## Research impacts

Coarsening occurs on Earth during the processing of any metal alloy and thus the coarsening process affects a large spectrum of products from dental fillings to turbine blades. Since the properties of an alloy are linked to the size of the particles within the solid, coarsening can be used to strengthen materials. This is the case with the majority of aluminum alloys used commercially today. Conversely, if the coarsening process proceeds too long the material can weaken. This occurs in jet turbine blades and is one of the reasons why turbine blades must be replaced after a certain number of hours of service. This investigation will provide insights into the dynamics of dendritic morphological evolution and will point the way to the formulation of models of this technologically important coarsening process. Thus developing accurate models of the coarsening process and validating them using benchmark experimental data is central to creating a wide range of new materials encompassing those used in automobiles to those used in space applications.
