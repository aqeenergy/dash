## Objective

InSPACE-2 studied the kinetics of chain formation in DC fields for suspensions of monodisperse, super-paramagnetic colloidal microspheres.  Objectives included:
1) To understand the percolation transition of MR suspensions in microgravity and the behavior of structures around this point (incipient structure).  The fluctuations of these chains can also be used to estimate the microrheological response of the MR fluid.  Additionally, measurements of the chain fluctuations in the final gelled material provide an estimate of the storage modulus of the suspension.
2) To determine the lowest energy configuration of 3D particle structures formed in a magnetorheological (MR) fluid in a pulsed magnetic field. Visual observations of microscopic structures will yield a better understanding of the interplay of the forces and interactions (magnetizing/demagnetizing, surface energy, and repulsion between structures) in MR suspensions. The most important dipolar interactions are the demagnetizing field, surface energy, and repulsion between structures.

## Hypothesis

The primary hypothesis is that energy barriers preventing colloidal phase transition can be overcome by changing the magnetic driving frequency and forces. In these gel systems, gravity plays a dominant role and would slowly compress and deform the gel structures when similar experiments are performed on Earth, whereas in space these structures can be maintained as long as the magnetic forces are applied.

## Approach

InSPACE-2 employed a microscopic video technique to study the internal microstructure in MR fluid suspensions. The investigation studied the assembly of magnetic particles in the presence of a toggled magnetic field. The microstructure of the suspension as the particles assemble was characterized by light microscopy. Samples for InSPACE-2 consisted of 1.05 μm diameter colloidal particles suspended in an aqueous phase. The particles were Dynabeads MyOne Carboxylic Acid from Invitrogen at four different concentrations, 0.40, 0.48, 0.56, 0.65%. When exposed to a continuous magnetic field each particle in the fluid acquires a magnetic dipole moment. Such dipoles mutually interact and align to form a particle chain structures. At sufficient concentrations and field strengths these chains form columns that cross-link to form an interconnected network.  The effects of sedimentation, which prohibits maintaining the particle concentration uniformity throughout the fluid, affect the study of MR fluids. Mechanical properties of the particle assemblies, especially buckling instabilities, will be characterized.

## Research impacts

The MR fluids studied in InSPACE-2 are a unique model system for studying the fundamental mechanisms of phase transitions and self-assembly. Suspension phase behavior affects the processing, stability and final properties of foods, personal care products, agrochemicals, paints, inks, pharmaceuticals and numerous other products and materials in which fine particles are suspended in fluids. Additionally, InSPACE-2 research emphasizes how to harness external fields in order to direct the assembly of novel micro- and nano-structured materials and devices. Such field-directed self-assembly represents a bottom-up approach to material and device fabrication, with the potential to dramatically reduce the cost of advanced technologies in photonics, heat transfer and photovoltaics. The MR response provides the basis for technologies ranging from actively controlled dampers and actuators to magnetically sealed bearings and sensitive stress transducers. MR fluids are currently used in wide range of applications, including active automotive shock absorbers, and engineers have explored their use in seismic dampers to protect large-scale structures during violent earthquakes. Applications for space exploration include their potential use in robots, rovers, and for mobility augmentation crew suits. Understanding the microstructural evolution of MR suspensions under time-varying conditions provides critical information for engineering and for tailoring their mechanical behavior.
