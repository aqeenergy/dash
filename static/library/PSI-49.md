# PSI-49 · DECLIC-ALI — DEvice for the study of Critical LIquids and Crystallization - Alice Like Insert

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Hardware:** Device for the Study of Critical Liquids and Crystallization (DECLIC)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Crystals · gas-liquid transformation · Crystallization · French Space Agency

## Objective

Identify and clarify the different growth mechanisms in fluid systems where hydrodynamics is relevant, particularly during phase separation near the critical point.

To investigate the role of fluctuating hydrodynamics (Brownian motion and local hydrodynamic interactions) in the coarsening of domains, especially when the equilibrium volume fraction of the new phase is minimal.

To analyze the growth kinetics of new experiments performed at very low volume fractions, taking into account fluctuation relaxation effects and Brownian motion within a hydrodynamic framework.

To investigate phase transitions induced by mechanical quenches (density variations using a piston), a novel approach contrasting with traditional temperature quenches.

To study the phase transition kinetics when the fluid is submitted to external vibration in low-g.

## Hypothesis

It is hypothesized that fluctuating hydrodynamics will dominate domain growth at low volume fractions, challenging classical theories. A sharp transition between growth regimes is expected. Mechanical quenches are hypothesized to be a viable method for inducing phase ordering. The vapor recoil force is believed to significantly influence bubble dynamics and wetting near the critical point. Adiabatic volume changes are expected to reveal the dynamics of the Piston Effect. Critical water is hypothesized to follow the same universality class, and external vibration will affect the dynamics.

## Approach

The investigation approach for DECLIC-ALI centers on conducting experiments within the microgravity environment of the ISS, utilizing the DECLIC facility and its specialized Alice-Like Insert (ALI, ALI-R, and ALI-2). The approach involves precise control of the temperature and density of the SF6 sample, including temperature and mechanical quenches, as well as heat pulse techniques. A suite of optical diagnostics (Interferometry, shadowgraphy, light scattering, and direct observation) is used to visualize and quantify the SF6 fluid behavior. Different sample cell configurations, including fixed and variable volume cells, are employed within the ALI insert. Data acquisition is performed at high frequencies, and the data are analyzed quantitatively, with a strong emphasis on comparing experimental results with theoretical predictions. The overall approach is iterative, building upon previous space-based experiments and refining both experimental techniques and theoretical models, all focused on understanding the critical phenomena of SF6.

## Research impacts

The DECLIC facility provides power, communications, command, control, data storage, and multiple, flexible optical capabilities in support of each experiment. The knowledge gained will assist in the understanding of the behavior of cryogenic (of or relating to very low temperatures) rocket propellants for the study of dynamics regarding near-ambient temperature critical fluids. DECLIC is designed for telescience from the ground and will offer scientists the capability to remotely control experiment conditions onboard the ISS provided by DECLIC lockers. The use of near-critical fluids allows important parameters (e.g., compressibility of supercritical fluids, density of gas and liquid phases, surface tension) to be easily varied in a scaled way by using small changes in temperature. The readily variable properties of near-critical fluids makes them appealing candidates for studying numerous interesting phenomena valid for all fluids, which will ultimately leads to the development of improved solvents for chemistry and the environment.
