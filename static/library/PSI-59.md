# PSI-59 · CCF-PS EU2 — Capillary Channel Flow - Phase Separation Experiment Unit 2

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Hardware:** Microgravity Science Glovebox
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Capillary Channels · CCF · Capillary Flow · Flow Rate · Capillary Channel Flow

## Objective

The objectives are to quantify phase separation efficiency in wedge channels as a function of flow parameters, bubble characteristics, and channel geometry; determine the influence of bubble size and injection frequency on separation efficiency; and optimize wedge geometry for passive phase separation.

## Hypothesis

The hypothesis underlying CCF-PS EU2 is that specific wedge and gapped-wedge channel designs can passively separate liquid and gas phases due to the complex interplay of capillary and inertial forces. Furthermore, it is hypothesized that by carefully adjusting flow conditions, such as flow rate and channel length as well as bubble properties, such as bubble size and frequency, it is possible to identify geometries and regimes suitable for gas-liquid separation, which can be used for spacecraft tank design and liquid supply.

## Approach

Building upon the CF-CV EU2 approach, the phase separation investigation utilizes a controlled bubble injection system to introduce bubbles of varying sizes and frequencies upstream of the wedge channel. The microgravity environment of the ISS ensures capillary-dominated flow conditions. Systematic variation of bubble injection parameters (size, frequency, location), flow rate, and channel geometry (wedge angle, length) enables comprehensive mapping of phase separation efficiency. High-speed imaging (HSHRC) coupled with wider-view video (MSGC) allows for detailed observation and quantification of bubble migration and coalescence. Image analysis techniques are employed to track bubble trajectories, quantify bubble size distributions before and after the wedge channel, and determine the overall degree of gas-liquid separation. This quantitative data is then correlated with the varied parameters to assess the effectiveness of the wedge geometry for passive phase separation and to optimize design parameters for enhanced performance.

## Research impacts

Current spacecraft fuel tanks rely on an additional reservoir to prevent gases from entering the engines during firing. Capillary systems larger than a few millimeters in length are impossible to study on the ground, but the weightlessness on the International Space Station makes research on a variety of capillary channel sizes possible. The results are useful for the design and testing of liquid management systems in current use and for future missions. Rocket fuel tank designs and construction models from space research help to advance new technology and manufacturing capabilities on Earth.
