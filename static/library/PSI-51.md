# PSI-51 · EML B1-ELFSTONE — Electromagnetic Levitation Flight Support for Transient Observation of Nucleation Events

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Hardware:** ESA ISS-EML Electromagnetic Levitator
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** ESA · alloys · solidification · Thermophysical Properties of Melts · modelling

## Objective

The objective of this project is to understand phase selection in structural alloys and to investigate the effects of convection on time-dependent nucleation phenomena during rapid solidification from the melt.  The ELFSTONE investigation involves a collaboration with the European Space Agency's (ESA) Thermolab Experiment which focuses on thermophysical properties data measurement of industrial alloys in the liquid phase as a function of temperature. The property values obtained are used to increase the accuracy of numerical modeling of casting and solidification processes of metal alloys.
For access to data relating to the Thermolab Experiment, please consult ESA's HRE Data Archive (hreda.esac.esa.int)

## Hypothesis

The experimental hypothesis was that melt convection would significantly decrease the nucleation kinetics for transformation of metastable ferrite to stable austenite in ferrous structural steel alloys. The was successfully observed.

## Approach

The samples are processed in the Electromagnetic Levitator (ISS-EML), a facility that has been operational since 2015 and is located in the Columbus module. The investigation is part of Sample Batch 1 - the first of six proposed 18-sample multi-user cartridges uploaded to the ISS.  For the ELFSTONE Sample #09, a ternary FeCrNi analog steel sample, a process cycle is defined as (1) the melting of the sample, (2) the gathering of science data, and (3) the subsequent undercooling and rapid solidification of the sample. The next cycle starts with the re-melting of the sample.  In any particular cycle, stirring may be induced at different levels to study the effect of convection on rapid solidification behavior.  After all cycles are complete, final microstructure is preserved for return to earth.

## Research impacts

The value of this research is that the results have application to the design of industrial welding, spray forming, and casting operations for a commercially important class of structural materials. In addition, this research addresses fundamental issues relating to rapid solidification behavior, metastable phase selection, and analysis of the processes governing microstructural evolution. Experiments focus on the use of convection as a processing control parameter.
