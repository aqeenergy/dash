# PSI-93 · PFMI — Pore Formation and Mobility Investigation

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Alloy Solidification
- **Hardware:** PFMI furnace
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Directional Solidification · thermocapillary convection · International Space Station · pore formation · microgravity · succinonitrile analogue

## Objective

The scientific objective of the investigation was to observe porosity formation and its movement. Methods to minimize porosity and its influence will also be examined. Experimental variables that was investigated include, but are not limited to, bubble size, bubble number, imposed temperature gradient, material properties, and the effect of applying vibration to the system. The role of ''spring and piston'' assemblages and in-situ thermocouples was also investigated and assessed.

## Hypothesis

Direct observation of pore/bubble formation and their mobility during processing in a microgravity environment will provide insight regarding the minimization of this detrimental feature during solidification processing of important metal alloys and semiconductors.

## Approach

The experimental approach consisted of employing a 10mm inside diameter quartz tubes on the order of 30 cm in length that was filled with pure succinonitrile (SCN) and SCN to which a small amount of water has been added to simulate alloy solidification. To investigate pore formation the sample tubes was filled by, at least, the three following methods. (1) Pieces of SCN was put in the tube. (2) SCN was cast into the tube. (3) SCN was cast into the tube and then be directionally solidified. Case 1 ensured porosity in the sample, case 2 allowed porosity to develop, and case 3 was prepared with the intent of minimizing porosity. Each sample tube was outfitted with an effective “spring and piston” assemblage and in-situ thermocouples. While thermocouples were required to accurately characterize the temperatures and temperature gradients they could also act as sites for pore nucleation and holding. The sample tubes were then inserted in a directional solidification furnace, which establishes a temperature gradient in the liquid and permits observation of the molten region. The furnace mounts on g-limit, a MSFC developed vibration isolation platform; this assemblage fits and was designed to operate within the ISS microgravity Glovebox. Porosity, which forms in the samples, was tracked and recorded with two video cameras set at 90 degrees from each other and orthogonal to the sample tube.

## Research impacts

PFMI provides insight on how materials solidify in the space environment. Once this process is understood and improvements are made, future manufacturing processes can take place in the microgravity environment providing robust products. On Earth, materials that contain pores created and trapped during solidification degrade properties and cause a distinct weakening in the overall structure of the cast product. Examples of these materials include semiconductors and aircraft turbine blades.
