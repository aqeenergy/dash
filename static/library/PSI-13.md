# PSI-13 · ACE-M1 — Advanced Colloids Experiment- Microscopy

- **Research area:** Complex Fluids - Soft Matter
- **Sub-area:** Colloids
- **Hardware:** Light Microscopy Module (LMM) Fluids Integrated Rack (FIR)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Polydispersity · Creams · Stabilization processes · Gels · Colloids

## Objective

The ACE-M1 investigation aims to study the behavior of microscopic particles in colloidal mixtures such as gels and creams.  Objectives include the study of the physics of colloidal coarsening and the role of polydispersity (variation in particle size) in determining the stability of colloidal systems. Previous Earth-based experiments show that polydispersity dramatically affects the time it takes for these gels to collapse. The imaging goal was to observe crystallites and resolve particle centroid positions with less than 20% error.

## Hypothesis

Polydispersity significantly affects the time scale and dynamics of coarsening. It is believed that polydispersity can lead to a substantial difference in the collapse time of weak gels, and understanding this effect in a microgravity environment can provide critical insights into product design and stability.

## Approach

The ACE-M1 investigation utilized a Sample Cartridge disk with 10 Sample Wells for 10 samples. Samples were colloidal fluids of small and large particles in varying concentration over .5 to 2.0 mg/ml.  Protocols included Homogenization and microscopic Observation and Imaging.  A crew member used  magnetic stir bars embedded in the sample well to homogenized each sample.  Fluorescent microscopy and Imaging of the colloidal crystallites in each well as they developed over time allowed observation of particle movements and interactions to characterize and quantify rates of growth of structures formed.

## Research impacts

Space Benefits: By conducting colloidal research in microgravity, the ACE-M1 experiment can obtain clearer data on the dynamics of liquid mixtures, free from the effects of gravitational forces. This research can improve the manufacturing processes for creating new, longer-lasting products for use both in space and on Earth.
Earth Benefits: Understanding the behavior of colloidal mixtures and the coarsening process can help materials scientists design products with longer shelf lives. This has significant implications for a wide range of products, including food, medicine, cosmetics, gels, and cleaning solutions, potentially leading to reduced waste and enhanced product performance​.
