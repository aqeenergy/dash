# PSI-97 · RSD-AFF — Ring-Sheared Drop - Amyloid Fibril Formation

- **Research area:** Biophysics
- **Sub-area:** Proteins
- **Hardware:** Ring Sheared Drop (RSD)
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** RSD · Shear-induced fibrillation · Fluid Dynamic Modeling · Biotechnology · Amyloid Fibril Formation · Protein Crystallization · Interfacial Shear · Proteins

## Objective

This campaign centered on four main objectives: (i) engineering the RSD to flight proven hardware, (ii) testing measurement and instrumentation methods aboard the ISS, (iii) studying protein kinetics under interfacial shear, and (iv) measuring and modeling protein solution rheology.

The primary obective of this investigation is to quantify the effects of flow on fibrillization in the bulk and at interfaces, specifically at the air/water interface. This includes studying protein kinetics under interfacial shear, and measuring and modeling protein solution rheology. The overall objective is project  to advance the mechanistic description of
fibrillization by determining how shearing flow affects each of the stages of amyloid
formation process − protein unfolding and nuclei formation, growth into protofibrils, and
elongation and intertwining into mature fibrils and fibril growth − without any influence
from solid boundaries.

## Hypothesis

The absence of gravity in a microgravity environment will significantly alter the amyloid fibril formation process in proteins.The hypothesis tested was that if protein solutions in microgravity are seeded with fibrils and subject to steady interfacial shear, then amyloidogenesis will occur with the extent of gelation depending on total protein concentration.

## Approach

The Ring Shear Drop (RSD) is used to facilitate amyloidogenesis and study flow dynamics without solid wall interactions. It uses surface tension to contain fluids in a microgravity environment. Techniques like Particle Tracking Velocimetry (PTV) are used to measure fluid dynamics and protein interactions. This information was used to create high-fidelity computational models.
Formation and flow of protein aggregates on the surface of the drop will be imaged by the microscope as a function of time. The RSD imaged the surface of a 2.50 cm diameter drop of protein solution with shear imposed by the rotation of one of the two sharp-edged rings (essentially knife edges) that constrain the drop.  A separate measure of the turbidity quantified by the extinction of a light beam through the center of the drop provided an independent signal for fibril formation. A variable degree of shear was imposed on the drop with a negligible contact with solid boundaries. Velocity of the fluid on the surface
of the drop will be determined by standard cross-correlation techniques from images obtained by the microscope. Details regarding sample composition and experimental parameters can be found in RSD Experimental Table.

## Research impacts

Amyloid fibril formation in proteins is of great interest because of its role in diseases such as Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s. For example, the waxy plaque choking off neurons in Alzheimer’s patients consists of the A-beta protein that has formed into amyloid fibrils. The microgravity environment aboard the ISS was utilized to study the biophysics of protein amyloidogenesis. The absence of gravity allows surface tension to provide fluid containment so that wall nucleation and other complications associated with solid containers can be avoided. Flow effects on protein fibrillization was studied at fluid interfaces similar to physiological processes, as all brain tissue consists of interfaces with fluidity, being comprised of cell membranes. The goal is to advance mechanistic descriptions of amyloid fibril formation.
