# PSI-89 · PBRE-WR — Packed Bed Reactor Experiment - Water Recovery

- **Research area:** Fluid Physics
- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Related investigations:** PSI-88 PSI-90
- **Keywords:** PBRE · Packed Bed Reactor Experiment · PBRE-WR

## Objective

The objective of the flight experiment was to study the hydrodynamics of packed beds in microgravity and under different gas and liquid flow conditions. The hydrodynamics consists of the pressure drop inside the packed bed and the liquid hold-up. The different regimes are the results of different liquid and gas flow rates, and different initial conditions embodied by gas and liquid pre-flow or flushes prior to beginning of the runs. Another objective of the experiment was to understand transient flow rates and the hysteresis in the pressure drop as a function of the flow rates.

## Hypothesis

While the overarching principles of two-phase flow in porous media apply, the specific material properties of alumina will cause unique flow phenomena, and models can be modified to account for such. Furthermore, this unique data is specifically relevant to the design and operation of reactors used in space-based water recovery systems, and the data can be used to improve and optimize such systems.

## Approach

The PBRE-WR investigations employed a systematic and comprehensive approach to study the hydrodynamics of two-phase flow in packed bed reactors under microgravity conditions. The approach can be summarized in the following key steps:

1. Ground-Based Research and Model Development:
Prior to the spaceflight experiments, extensive ground-based research was conducted to develop theoretical models and establish a foundation for understanding two-phase flow in packed beds. This included:
   Literature review: A thorough review of existing literature on single-phase and two-phase flow through porous media, focusing on pressure drop, flow regimes, and the influence of various parameters.
   Development of hydrodynamic models: Formulation of mathematical models, such as the modified Ergun equation, to predict pressure drop and flow regime transitions in microgravity.
   Ground-based experiments: Conducting experiments in reduced gravity environments, such as parabolic flights and drop towers, to gather preliminary data and validate initial model predictions.

2. Design and Development of Spaceflight Experiment:
A dedicated experimental facility was designed and developed for operation on the ISS. This involved:
   Test facility design: Engineering a flow control system, test section, and data acquisition system capable of operating reliably in microgravity and meeting the specific requirements of the investigation.
   Selection of packing materials: PBRE-WR used 3.5 mm Alumina packing, a common inert material used in reactor beds such as water purification systems.
   Definition of test matrix: Developing a comprehensive test matrix encompassing a wide range of gas and liquid flow rates, pre-flow conditions, and transient flow scenarios.

3. Execution of Spaceflight Experiments:
The PBRE-WR experiments were conducted on the ISS, following the defined test matrix and procedures. This involved:
   Preflow procedures: Implementing the chosen pre-flow treatment (liquid flush or gas flush) before each test run.
   Flow control: Precisely controlling and varying the gas and liquid flow rates according to the test matrix.
   Data acquisition: Collecting data from pressure sensors, temperature sensors, and high-speed cameras throughout each test run.
   Downlinking data: Transmitting the acquired data to the ground for analysis.

4. Data Analysis and Interpretation:
The downlinked data was meticulously analyzed to:
   Characterize flow regimes: Identify and classify the different flow regimes observed in the experiments based on pressure traces and video recordings.
   Evaluate pressure drop: Analyze the pressure drop data and compare it with model predictions to assess the validity and accuracy of the models.
   Investigate flow regime transitions: Determine the conditions and mechanisms leading to transitions between different flow regimes.
   Assess the influence of packing properties and pre-flow conditions: Analyze the impact of packing material wettability, size, and pre-flow treatments on the flow behavior and pressure drop.

5. Dissemination of Results:
The findings of the PBRE-WR investigations have not been completed and disseminated yet.

## Research impacts

Fundamental understanding the impact of reduced or zero gravity on the hydrodynamics (pressure drop, as well as distribution and holdup of each phase) is relevant to systems that involve two-phase flow such as life support systems, fuel cells, in-situ resource utilization, heat pipes, materials processing, production of pharmaceutical-grade water, and transport of nutrients in soils. Design and operation of many chemical and biological processing systems require this fundamental understanding of two-phase gas-liquid flows through porous media because many of these space-based systems or unit operations involve gas-liquid flows through porous media. Some examples of current space systems involving two-phase flow through fixed porous media include the Volatile Removal Assembly (VRA), the Integrated Advanced Water Recovery System (AWRS), and IntraVenous Water GENeration system (IVGEN). The ability to predict flow regimes and corresponding pressure drop in a particular system involving two-phase flow is crucial to the design of the fluid system and the pumping requirements.
