# PSI-40 · CSLM-2 — Coarsening in Solid Liquid Mixtures-2

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Solidification, Coarsening
- **Hardware:** CSLM Sample Processing Unit (SPU) and Electronic Control Unit (ECU)
- **Flight platform:** ISS
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** Ostwald Ripening · Microgravity · Coarsening · Kinetics

## Objective

The objective of the CSLM-2 investigation was essentially the same as the original CSLM investigation with the addition of higher solid volume percent  samples requiring longer times to reach steady state coarsening rates.

## Hypothesis

The theory of coarsening has been described by Lifshitz, Slyozov and Wagner (LSW) predicts that for the limit of zero volume fraction particles grow with time as the cube root of the average radius, and the particle size distribution becomes independent of time. The investigation hypothesizes that finding a system which satisfies all the constraints of LSW theory such as particles are infinitely separated and there is no diffusional interaction between them is prohibitive for ground-based laboratory condition due to effects of particle sedimentation and buoyancy-induced convection and therefore the theory can only be tested by performing experiments in a microgravity environment, For CSLM-2 it is hypothesized that for large volume fraction solid samples the transient coarsening process will be longer and steady state will be reached much later compared to low volume fraction solid particles.

## Approach

The CSLM-2 approach and hardware was identical to the predecessor CSLM investigation. The solid volume fraction of the 4 samples for CSLM-2 were 0.3. 0.5, 0.7, and 0.8.

## Research impacts

Coarsening occurs on Earth during the processing of any metal alloy and thus the coarsening process affects a large spectrum of products from dental fillings to turbine blades. Since the properties of an alloy are linked to the size of the particles within the solid, coarsening can be used to strengthen materials. This is the case with the majority of aluminum alloys used commercially today. Conversely, if the coarsening process proceeds too long the material can weaken. This occurs in jet turbine blades and is one of the reasons why turbine blades must be replaced after a certain number of hours of service. Thus developing accurate models of the coarsening process is central to creating a wide range of new materials encompassing those used in automobiles to those used in space applications.
