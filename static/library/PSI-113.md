# PSI-113 · SUBSA — Solidification Using a Baffle in Sealed Ampoules

- **Research area:** Materials Science
- **Sub-area:** Solidification, Crystal growth
- **Hardware:** SUBSA Furnace
- **Flight platform:** ISS
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation
- **Keywords:** InSb · Baffle · Semiconductor Crystals · Segregation · Microgravity · Directional Solidification · Magnetic Field · Bridgman Configuration

## Objective

There are both technology demonstration objectives and scientific objectives. The technology demonstration objectives are to test the performance of an automatically moving baffle in microgravity and to determine the behavior and possible advantages of liquid encapsulation in microgravity conditions.The science objectives are to visualize the melt/encapsulant behavior in microgravity and to grow doped InSb without dewetting. The key goal of SUBSA was to clarify the origin of the melt convection in space and to reduce the magnitude to the point that it does not interfere with the transport phenomena.

## Hypothesis

A baffle placed inside a melt reduces the effective size of the melt and leads to a decrease in the amount of convection. The combined effect of microgravity (reduction of gravitational acceleration by a factor of 10^6 ) and a small zone melt (reduction of the size and temperature differential by a factor of 10^3 ) will generate the effective conditions of “nano-gravity” (the Rayleigh number will be reduced by a factor of 10^9 ).

## Approach

In the space environment, accelerations produce circular motions that affect the solidification of crystal growth&#x2F;semiconductor materials flight experiments, resulting in a material that is less useful.The objectives of SUBSA are:Test the performance of an automatically moving baffle (used to minimize melt motion).Visualization of the melting process to identify what causes the melt motion.Production of several crystals with reproducible properties.To determine the behavior and possible advantages of liquid encapsulation in microgravity conditions.

## Research impacts

The SUBSA furnace, with its capability to control and visualize melting and solidification of semiconductor crystals has increased our understanding of semiconductor crystal growth phenomena. The transparent furnace coupled with the video downlink and real-time commanding capability provided a powerful tool for scientists to watch the motion of the crystal-melt interface as semiconductor crystals were formed. The design of the SUBSA ampoule which includes a baffle and a system that prevents de-wetting resulted in crystals whose properties have not been disrupted by micro-accelerations at the Station, making it possible for future researchers to produce the high-quality semiconductor crystals that are in demand on Earth.
