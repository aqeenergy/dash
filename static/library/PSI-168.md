# PSI-168 · ACE-T10 — Advanced Colloids Experiment - Aging Dynamics and Stress-Relaxation in Colloidal Gels Studied in Microgravity

- **Research area:** Complex Fluids - Soft Matter
- **Sub-area:** Colloids

- **Flight platform:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Project type:** Microgravity Investigation

## Objective

Colloidal stability is critical to soft matter systems, especially to consumer products. These structures change in time, by processes know as coarsening - meaning the spacing between particles compact. This compaction continually changes this structure. When the structures created in the products can longer resist the gravitational stresses (e.g. buoyancy) on the structures, they collapse. The collapse, such as those that occur in geological formations during earthquakes, is often abrupt and without warning (delayed collapse). This is the essence of product instability. For some systems, the gel collapse can be anticipated by precursors, that can potentially be detected and forecasted.

This research will have significant impacts in two areas:
     •	It is not clear how the microscopic structure and its dynamics are related to the gel mechanical properties. The structure evolution controls the aging of the gel, and, on earth, this eventually leads to collapse. This work may significantly enhance the knowledge necessary to advance this important area of science.
     •	Second, relaxation of localized stresses in an amorphous solid brought by a rapid solidification process is ubiquitous in nature and can damage and destroy the materials. Besides affecting the rheological properties of the materials, these dynamics rule the aging of the thermal systems at rest. The detection of failure precursors, as the ones discovered in our colloidal gel, can potentially allow us to forecast the material failure before catastrophic rupture.

## Hypothesis

ACE-T10 delivered unique insights into the restructuring behavior of thermo-reversible colloidal gels, a topic of noticeable interest for the community of scientists dealing with soft materials. In normal gravity experiments, the ACE-T10 science team found evidence that the aging of these soft gels proceed via a sequence of sudden intermittent bursts of dynamical activity that resembles the ‘critical avalanche’ phenomena observed in several driven systems. The evidence collected suggests that, in a soft solid, these avalanches may be triggered by thermal agitation and occur spontaneously, even in the absence of other external forces. However, because of the density difference between the fluid and the particles in the gel network, gravity may play a role as well. Performing the ACE-T10 experiment in microgravity provided a unique opportunity to determine the gravity contribution.

## Approach

The ACE-T10 experiment exploited the Fluids Integrated Rack (FIR) and the Light Microscopy Module (LMM). The Light Microscopy Module (LMM) is an automated microscope. It permitted flexible imaging (e.g., bright field, dark field, phase contrast, confocal microscopy) for physics experiments and can be controlled remotely from the ground. The LMM accommodated sample change-out and fluid containment and included a glove box for on-orbit sample manipulation.

The ACE-T10 experiment used include confocal microscopy, which utilized a 532-nm frequency-doubled Nd:YAG laser, a confocal scanner, and an 8- to 12-bit digital CCD camera. The scanner captured 30 frames per second of confocal images by the CCD camera. A sample’s three-dimensional crystal structure can thus be reconstructed by assembling individual slices with an image analysis program. This information, in turn, enabled the observation and measurement of the colloidal growth, structure, and dynamics. The confocal module was attached and aligned to the side of the LMM. The microscope used a reflecting mirror to direct the light to and from the sample.

ACE-T10 utilized 6 capillary cells in 2 holding modules. The capillary cells, each contained about 20 microliters of the sample fluid and a magnetic stainless steel stir bar. Samples were stirred prior to example. The sample temperature was adjusted between 20 – 60 °C in order to find the transition temperature where the sample became a gel.

## Research impacts

Earth benefits/spin-off applications include understanding the behavior of weak gels and their aging can help materials scientists to make consumer product with longer shelf lives. In addition, because several features of the failure dynamics of disordered materials are system independent,  the results obtained using this model colloidal system can be generalized to other materials. This potentially can help to develop new way to characterize aging material and suggest their replacement before catastrophic failure.
